### Overview

This assignment is in two parts: 

**Special Interest Group (SIG) Presentation** 

- Group (3-4 students)
- In-class, 20 mins (maximum)
- Schedule: Session A: Fri 4 Dec; Session B: Fri 11 Dec
- **Deadline**: Session A: Fri 4 Dec; Session B: Fri 11 Dec
- Grade format: collective, Pass/Fail
- Grade weight: 15%

**Special Interest Group (SIG) Paper**

- 1,000 words (approx.), 4 pages double-spaced, with bibliography, not included in word count) 
- **Deadline**: Fri 11 Dec. Session A groups have an automatic extension until Mon 14 Dec.
- Grade format: individual, letter
- Grade weight: 15%

***

### Format

**Special Interest Group (SIG) Presentation**

- A list of possible focus topics is provided, but your group may also choose a different focus as long as it relates to course topics;
- Each group is responsible for organising itself in terms of allocation of responsibilities (research, scope of presentation, presentation materials (slides, clips, etc.), speakers, and collectively planning the presentation itself;
- Since the presentation itself is short (20-minute time-slot), not everyone in the group has to speak, as long as other members contribute equally to the presentation;
- On the day of the session (A or B) designated for your presentation, make sure that you are ready to plug in quickly on a single laptop of a group member and have materials ready to present; switching between laptops will limit time available for your presentation;
- As a courtesy to other groups, please be sure to limit your presentation to 20 minutes.

**Special Interest Group (SIG) Paper**

- After your presentation, write up an analytical reflection on your topic in an individual critical essay of 1,000 words (4 pages, double-spaced) in length.
- The assignment is an **analysis** paper; it should therefore not simply consist of a narrative or presentation of information. Background information should be included, especially in the introduction, but should not account for the entirety of the paper itself.
- Your primary objective should be to demonstrate your familiarity with and understanding of the history of interactive media, and where your topic fits into that history, based on your engagement with reading and screening assignments in the course.
- This does not simply mean inserting direct quotations (which you should only use if you wish to discuss or call attention to a specific phrase or term in the source), but actively contextualizing and situating your topic within the larger history of interactive media.
- Analytical and conceptual questions to discuss in relation to your research topic may be based on reading assignments, but may relate to questions such as historical significance; technical affordances or limitations; cultural issues, including race/ethnicity/gender/class identities.

***

### Paper format and style

- 1,000 words (max. 1,250 words)
- No cover sheet
- Descriptive title (Important: do not simply use the generic name of the assignment as the title), with optional subtitle
- Standard 12-point font, black (serif preferred, e.g. Times New Roman, Garamond, Palatino)
- Double spacing
- Header including your name, course number and title, instructor name, date
- Number pages
- Works Cited section at end (**not included in word count**)
- In Works Cited section, online sources must be linked (do not post URLs)
- Accuracy of grammar and punctuation is expected - be sure to proof-read your paper before submitting it. Papers with large numbers of  careless errors and typos will be penalized at least one grade increment 
- Style should follow that of published sources in reading assignments: do not use conversational expressions, slang, SMS punctuation
